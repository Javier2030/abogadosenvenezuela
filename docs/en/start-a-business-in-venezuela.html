How to start a business in Venezuela as a foreigner (2026)

Yes, a foreigner can own a company in Venezuela, and in most cases set it up without living there. This guide explains what the law actually requires, what it costs, how long it takes and where foreign investors usually go wrong — with the official source for every figure.

By Abogados en Venezuela · reviewed by Abg. Adelaida Victoria Mata de Pérez, lawyer licensed in Venezuela · updated 11 September 2026

The short answer

  • Vehicle: a Compañía Anónima (C.A.), incorporated with at least two shareholders.
  • Capital: no legal minimum; it must be fully subscribed and at least 20% of each shareholder's shares paid in.
  • Official cost: about 3% of the declared capital in registry fees and tax, plus small fixed fees.
  • Time: 5–10 business days for the incorporation itself, once the foreign shareholders' pre-registration is approved (that step has been reported to take from two weeks to several months).
  • No need to travel for most steps: a power of attorney with apostille (or consular legalization) lets a Venezuelan attorney act for you.

Venezuela in 2026: what has changed

Venezuela is reopening to foreign trade. The Hydrocarbons Law was reformed in January 2026, direct flights with the United States resumed in April and with Spain (Iberia and Air Europa) in September, and day-to-day business already runs largely on US dollars. Inflation is still very high (over 500% year-on-year in August 2026, according to the Central Bank), so prices and contracts are normally agreed in dollars.

Two realities matter for planning:

  • Sanctions are still in force. The United States keeps the Government of Venezuela and PDVSA under sanctions (OFAC), and the European Union keeps restrictive measures on listed persons (extended to 10 January 2027). Private business is allowed, but you must screen customers and counterparties, especially state-owned ones.
  • Infrastructure. After the double earthquake of 24 June 2026, Maiquetía airport operates at roughly half of its capacity, a situation expected to last into 2027; plan trips and logistics with that in mind.

Can a foreigner own 100% of a Venezuelan company?

Yes. There is no general nationality requirement for shareholders. A foreigner can own and manage a Venezuelan company, and to register as a shareholder a passport is enough.

The exceptions are sector-specific. Road transport of passengers and cargo is reserved to Venezuelans and resident foreigners (Land Transport Law, art. 100), and hydrocarbons, mining, banking, insurance, telecommunications and media require specific authorizations. If your business falls in one of these sectors, the structure has to be designed before incorporating; fixing it afterwards means amending the articles, paying the registry again and losing months.

Which type of company to choose

  • Compañía Anónima (C.A.) — the standard vehicle, regulated by the Commercial Code. Shareholders' liability is limited to their contribution.
  • Sociedad de Responsabilidad Limitada (S.R.L.) — still in the Code, but its statutory capital ceiling (art. 315) was never updated after the currency redenominations, which makes it impractical today.
  • Firma personal — a sole proprietorship for an individual who operates alone; it does not separate personal and business assets.
  • Branch of a foreign company — possible, but the foreign company must register in Venezuela and appoint a representative (Commercial Code, arts. 354–355), and a branch has no separate legal personality: the parent answers directly for its Venezuelan liabilities.

For almost every foreign investor, the answer is a C.A.

Three myths you will read in other guides

“One shareholder is enough.” The Civil Code defines a company as a contract between two or more persons (art. 1649), and in practice the Mercantile Registry incorporates a C.A. with at least two shareholders. Afterwards, one shareholder may end up holding all the shares without dissolving the company (Commercial Code, art. 341).

“The minimum capital is X dollars.” The Commercial Code sets no minimum capital for a C.A. What does exist is a reasonableness check: the registrar can reject a capital that is clearly insufficient for the business described (Registries and Notaries Law, art. 57.1).

“You must appoint a resident local director.” No rule requires a Venezuelan shareholder. What you do need, in practice, is someone in Venezuela for the in-person steps — a partner, a representative or your attorney-in-fact — and a foreign director may need the appropriate visa.

Capital: the rules

  • The capital must be 100% subscribed, and at least 20% of each shareholder's shares must be paid in at incorporation (Commercial Code, art. 249). It is a minimum per shareholder, not a global one.
  • In-kind contributions (vehicles, equipment, software) are accepted with a valuation by an independent expert (Registries and Notaries Law, art. 57.2).
  • Declared capital is the tax base of the registration fees, so every extra dollar declared costs about three cents on day one. How much to declare, and how to fund the rest, depends on your plan and should be decided before incorporating.

Documents and signatures

  • Passport of each foreign shareholder.
  • Pre-registration of each foreign shareholder in SAREN's foreign registry (sisref.saren.gob.ve). This is usually the bottleneck.
  • A power of attorney signed in your country, with apostille if your country is part of the Hague Convention or consular legalization if it is not, endorsed by a Venezuelan lawyer.
  • A sworn statement of the origin and destination of funds (SAREN Resolution 008, art. 27). The power of attorney must expressly allow your attorney to sign it.
  • Biometric identification (fingerprint, photo and electronic signature) of whoever signs at the registry, in place since SAREN Providencia 525 (Official Gazette 42.987, October 2024). Someone signs in person in Venezuela: you, a partner or your attorney-in-fact.
  • The articles of association must be drafted and endorsed by a lawyer licensed in Venezuela (Law of Lawyers, art. 6; Registries and Notaries Law, art. 23).

Taxes at a glance

  • Corporate income tax (ISLR): 15% up to 2,000 tax units, 22% between 2,000 and 3,000, and 34% above (Income Tax Law, Official Gazette 6.210 Ext., 2015).
  • Dividends: 34% applies only to the part of the profit that has not already paid income tax (the “excess dividend”, arts. 65–67), and tax treaties reduce it — with Spain, for example, to 0% or 10%.
  • VAT: 16% general rate, with exemptions such as domestic passenger transport (VAT Law, art. 19.1).
  • IGTF: 3% on payments in foreign currency made to special taxpayers or within the Venezuelan banking system (Official Gazette 6.687 Ext., 25 February 2022).
  • Municipal tax on gross income, at a rate that depends on the municipality and the activity — for road transport in Caracas, from 1% (Libertador) to 1.5% (Chacao and Baruta).

Foreign investment registration and bringing your money back

The Constitutional Law on Productive Foreign Investment (Official Gazette 41.310, 29 December 2017) sets a minimum of €800,000 to register a foreign investment (art. 19), which can be lowered to no less than 10%. Below that amount you can incorporate and operate normally (art. 38), but you do not obtain the foreign investor certificate nor the statutory right to repatriate capital and dividends (arts. 25 and 28).

That is why capital and financing must be planned before incorporating, not after. Check also whether your country has a bilateral investment treaty with Venezuela — Spain's, for example, has been in force since 10 September 1997 — because it protects your investment regardless of that registration.

Work visa, residence and citizenship

Your company can support your work visa; the exact requirements depend on your passport and are checked case by case. Over time, residence can lead to Venezuelan citizenship: the Constitution requires at least 10 years of continuous residence, reduced to 5 years for nationals of Spain, Portugal, Italy, Latin America and the Caribbean (art. 33).

Opening a bank account

Venezuelan banks apply enhanced due diligence to companies with foreign shareholders, and some nationalities take longer. It is often the slowest step after the pre-registration, so start preparing the file early and do not promise suppliers a date before the account is open.

Selling to the Venezuelan State

A new company can register in the National Contractors Registry (RNC) with an opening balance; no track record is required. In 2026 most public demand comes from the oil sector — PDVSA and its joint ventures — so every contracting entity must be screened against OFAC and EU sanctions before you bid, and payments of that origin should never be mixed with your bank accounts abroad.

The most expensive mistakes

  • A business purpose that does not match the permits you will need: amending it later means a new shareholders' meeting, a new registration and new fees.
  • Choosing a domicile where you do not operate: municipal tax and local preferences follow the real place of business.
  • Declaring more capital than needed, or less than the registrar will accept.
  • Ignoring the reserved sectors until the permit is refused.
  • Having no plan to bring money back below the €800,000 threshold.
  • Contracting with sanctioned entities without screening them first.

Frequently asked questions

Can a foreigner start a business in Venezuela?

Yes. A foreigner can own and manage a Venezuelan company. Only some sectors, such as road transport of people and cargo, are reserved to Venezuelans and resident foreigners, and others need specific authorizations.

Can I set up a company in Venezuela without traveling?

In most cases, yes. You sign a power of attorney in your country, with apostille or consular legalization, and a Venezuelan attorney acts for you. Someone must sign in person at the registry, which can be your attorney-in-fact.

How much does it cost to register a company in Venezuela?

The official fees are about 3% of the declared capital, plus small fixed fees and the professional fees of a lawyer and a public accountant. Our turnkey packages cost US$5,000 or US$6,000, paid by phases.

What is the minimum capital for a C.A.?

There is no legal minimum. The capital must be fully subscribed and at least 20% of each shareholder's shares paid in, and the registrar can reject a capital that is clearly insufficient for the business.

How long does it take?

The incorporation takes 5–10 business days once the foreign shareholders' pre-registration is approved; that pre-registration has been reported to take from two weeks to several months.

Do I need a Venezuelan partner?

Not as a general rule. A company can be 100% foreign-owned, except in reserved sectors. You will need someone in Venezuela for the in-person steps.

Can I get a work visa through my company?

Yes. Your company can support your work visa; the requirements depend on your passport and are checked in advance.

Can I send profits abroad?

Yes, but below the €800,000 threshold for registering a foreign investment there is no statutory repatriation right, so financing and profit distribution should be planned before incorporating.
